# Bug Audit — 2026-07-28 (post-bugfix codebase, Fable)

Scope: full audit of `src/` and `tests/` **as they stand after the 17
fixes** recorded in `primer.md` (A4, A5, A8, F1–F10). This is a fresh pass,
not a re-verification of the previous audit. Primary question driving it:
**what could produce the unresolved "latency/offset feels too large"
playtest report** that `Swinger_Build_Plan_v3.md` Phase 0 exists to close
out — plus everything else found along the way.

Method: static review of all 9 modules; ran all 5 offline tests (all
PASS: pygame 2.6.1, dummy SDL drivers); replayed the three real captures
in `src/` (`swings.csv`, `swings_fresh.csv`, `swings_counted.csv`) through
the current detector and scorer; and checked the timing arithmetic of the
judge/grace/window/render paths numerically.

Prior fixes re-checked in passing and holding: A4 (`max_measures` guard),
A5/F3 (unified 200Hz poll + duplicate skip; fresh captures show 66.2Hz
with 2–3 residual duplicates out of ~1,300–2,000 samples), F2
(`max_derivative` computed and threaded), F4 (non-blocking popup; the
500-measure probe captures 500 wind-up samples), F5/F6 (calibration
save-guard and matched-only seed), F7 (offset-centred window). **No
regressions found in the previously fixed items.** Everything below is new.

Severity: **P1** = plausibly causes the reported latency complaint or
silently corrupts scoring; **P2** = real defect, narrower blast radius;
**P3** = correctness/robustness/hygiene.

## P1 — Latency complaint: three additive causes, all in `game.py`

These are listed first because together they fully explain a "too laggy to
tell if scoring works" report **without needing any hardware to confirm**.
Arithmetic below uses the shipped defaults (80 BPM, 2/4, `offset = 0.05`).

### G1 (P1). Every judgment is withheld ~675ms past the downbeat, even when the result was already decided
`MeasureJudge.tick()` refuses to judge until
`window_end + judge_grace_s`, where
`window_end = beat1 + offset + beat_interval/2`:

```
window_end   = beat1 + 0.05 + 0.375 = beat1 + 0.425 s
judge fires ≥ beat1 + 0.425 + 0.25  = beat1 + 0.675 s
```

So a player who swings *exactly* on the click waits **~625ms** to find out
what happened, every single measure. That alone reads as "huge latency."

The important part: **the outcome is already fully determined the moment
`_locked_event` is set.** `submit_ictus()` locks the *first* in-window
event and explicitly ignores all later ones, and `_judge()` reads nothing
but `_locked_event`. Waiting for `window_end + grace` after a lock cannot
change the result — it only delays it.

**Fix direction:** judge immediately when `_locked_event is not None`;
keep the `window_end + grace` wait *only* for the no-event (Miss) case,
which is the only branch that genuinely needs to wait to be sure nothing
arrives. Expected felt latency drops from ~625ms to the detector pipeline
lag alone (~170–200ms, see G2). This is a small change in `tick()` and is
the single highest-value fix in this audit.

### G2 (P1). `JUDGE_GRACE_S` is a fixed 250ms that (a) is larger than the worst case it protects against and (b) silently breaks above 120 BPM
The grace exists to cover detector delivery lag. That lag is bounded by
`MIN_SEEK_TIMEOUT_S` (150ms) + smoothing lag (~23ms at 66Hz) + one sample
(~15ms) ≈ **190ms worst case**, and is far smaller in normal play because
a real swing's rebound finalizes `POST_DROP_SEEK_MIN` almost immediately.
250ms is therefore over-budgeted on every measure.

Worse, it is a **constant not scaled to tempo**. The inter-window gap is
`beat_interval/2`, so the grace is only safe while
`JUDGE_GRACE_S < 30/BPM` — i.e. **BPM < 120**. At or above 120 BPM the
grace period extends past the *next* measure's window opening; because
`submit_ictus()` always tests against `self.measure_index`'s window (which
hasn't advanced yet), genuinely in-window swings for measure N+1 get
filed as wind-up samples and are lost, producing phantom Misses. `bpm` is
a config field, so this is one config edit away from a confusing bug.

**Fix direction:** derive the grace from the detector's own bound
(`min_seek_timeout_s` + smoothing lag) rather than hardcoding 250ms, and
clamp it to `beat_interval/2` with an assert or a warning. Combined with
G1 it stops mattering on the common path.

### G3 (P1). The display runs at ~13 fps and the popup can appear up to 75ms late — render cadence is tied to sample *count*, not time
`if frame_counter % 5 == 0:` draws once every 5 samples. The comment
assumes a fast sample stream, but F3's duplicate-skip means the loop
iterates at the *device* rate (measured 66.2Hz in both fresh captures),
not the 200Hz poll rate. Every-5th-sample at 66Hz is:

```
5 / 66.2 Hz = 75.5 ms per frame ≈ 13.2 fps
```

Everything visual — popups, the background clear, any future ball/batter
animation — updates at 13fps, and each judgment popup appears up to 75ms
after it was decided. On top of G1 this is exactly the "feels unresponsive
/ delayed" texture of the complaint, and it would be easy to misattribute
to scoring latency.

**Fix direction:** drive rendering off elapsed wall-clock time (draw if
`now - last_draw >= 1/60`), not a modulo on sample count.

## P1 — Scoring correctness

### S1 (P1). `FALLBACK_SHARPNESS_LOW_HIGH` is derived from two mutually incompatible captures; pre-calibration bucketing is effectively arbitrary
`scoring.py` documents the range `(60000, 420000)` as a p10–p90 trim over
"53 detected events" from the two post-F3 captures. Replaying both files
through the current detector:

| capture | events | `max_derivative` min / median / max |
|---|---|---|
| `swings_fresh.csv` (66.2Hz) | 23 | 65,907 / **312,698** / 424,199 |
| `swings_counted.csv` (66.1Hz) | 29 | 9,960 / **65,227** / 960,977 |

The two medians differ by ~4.8×: `swings_fresh`'s median sits near
`swings_counted`'s *maximum* region, and `swings_counted`'s median sits at
`swings_fresh`'s *minimum*. Pooling them and taking p10–p90 averages away
a bimodality rather than describing a distribution. With the shipped
range, the bucket cuts land at ≤180k = Low, ≤300k = Mid, >300k = High —
which means a `swings_fresh`-style session buckets almost everything
**High** (every hit a Home Run) and a `swings_counted`-style session
buckets almost everything **Low** (every hit a Bunt). The sharpness axis
is still not doing useful work before first calibration; it's just failing
in a less obvious way than the old 150–600 placeholder did.

Also: my replay counts **52** events (23+29), not the 53 the comment
claims, and **29** events for `swings_counted`, not the 30 the Phase 4
note claims. Small, but it means the recorded numbers were produced
against a different code state than what's checked in — worth
re-deriving rather than trusting.

**Fix direction:** don't pool captures with different swing regimes.
Either derive the fallback from one controlled capture that deliberately
spans soft→hard at a steady cadence, or (better) make the fallback
*relative* — bucket the first N live swings against their own running
distribution and only fall back to absolutes for the first 2–3 swings.

### S2 (P1). `max_derivative` is a max-over-per-sample-ratios, so it is maximally sensitive to sample-interval jitter
`raw_sharpness()` returns `max_derivative` whenever it's set (the normal
path), and the detector computes it as `max((mag−prev)/dt)`. Measured dt
in the real captures:

```
swings_fresh.csv   dt ms: p05 10.82  med 16.34  p95 17.27  max 48.45
swings_counted.csv dt ms: p05 10.93  med 16.46  p95 17.27  max 85.50
```

A ~1.6× spread in dt between p05 and p95 feeds directly into the metric as
a ~1.6× spread in the reported sharpness, independent of how the player
actually swung — and because it's a **max**, the statistic deliberately
selects the sample pair with the smallest dt, i.e. the noisiest one. That
noise is then fed into a three-way percentile bucketing, where a 1.6×
error easily crosses a bucket boundary. This is a plausible contributor to
S1's wild per-capture spread.

**Fix direction:** make the sharpness numerator robust — e.g. the largest
magnitude *increase over a fixed ~30–50ms window* rather than the largest
single-interval ratio, or the median of the top-k per-sample rates.
Whatever is chosen must be re-derived into S1's fallback range.

## P2 — Real defects with narrower blast radius

### J1 (P2). Settings → Calibrate opens a **second concurrent HID connection** to the same Joy-Con without closing the first
`JoyConStream` has no `close()`, `__del__`, or context-manager support,
and nothing ever releases the device. `run_game()` constructs one, then
after the round `run_settings_menu()` → `run_calibration()` calls the
factory again and constructs a **second** `JoyCon` on the same physical
device while the first handle is still open. On Windows HID this is a
classic source of "calibration silently reads nothing" or an outright
open failure — and it lands precisely on the code path the player is
directed to when timing feels wrong. Untested to date (the menu flow has
never been exercised end-to-end on hardware).

**Fix direction:** give `JoyConStream` a `close()` and make it a context
manager; pass the *existing* stream into the settings menu instead of
re-opening via the factory, or close it before handing off.

### J2 (P2). The whole game loop is driven by sensor samples, so a still controller or a dropped connection stalls or crashes the round
`run_game()` is `for ... in joycon_stream.stream(...)`, and `stream()` now
*skips* duplicate readings entirely. Consequences:

- **Stall:** if the device returns an unchanged report (controller set
  down, BT hiccup), the loop yields nothing → `judge.tick()` is never
  called → no judging, no popup, no QUIT handling, no game-over. The
  window freezes and the round hangs until the 750s stream duration
  expires. The dedup should suppress *feeding the detector*, not suppress
  the loop iteration.
- **Crash:** `read_sample()` has no error handling; a mid-round disconnect
  raises out of the generator, killing the process and losing the entire
  `SessionLog` (which is never written to disk anyway — see H3).

**Fix direction:** always iterate on a timer; pass duplicates through to
the loop but skip `detector.process_sample()` for them. Wrap
`read_sample()` and surface a device-lost state on screen.

### C1 (P2). F6's fix is incomplete: timing-outlier swings still seed the sharpness distribution, and the ≥3 guard counts the wrong set
In `run_calibration()`, `matched_events` is every per-measure closest
event *before* `compute_offset()`'s ±half-beat outlier filter. Two
consequences:

- The sharpness seed is built from `matched_events`, so a wildly
  mistimed swing that `compute_offset()` correctly rejected for the offset
  is still admitted into the sharpness reference — the exact class of
  contamination F6 was meant to remove.
- The save guard `if result.from_default or len(matched_events) < 3:`
  tests the pre-filter set. A calibration where only one swing survived
  the outlier bound but three matched loosely will still overwrite
  `calibration.json` — a weaker guarantee than F5 intended.

**Fix direction:** have `compute_offset()` return the indices/events it
actually used, and drive both the seed and the ≥3 guard off that set.

### D1 (P2). `POST_DROP_SEEK_MIN` uses `<=`, so a plateau drags the reported ictus timestamp later
`if mag <= self._seeking_min_val` keeps advancing `_seeking_min_t`
whenever the smoothed magnitude merely *ties* the running minimum. On a
flat bottom the reported ictus time slides to the end of the plateau
rather than its start, adding a variable positive bias to the very
timestamp the whole game scores against. Rare with float magnitudes, and
partially masked because calibration absorbs a *constant* bias — but this
one isn't constant, it varies with how flat the bottom of the swing is
(i.e. with swing style).

**Fix direction:** strict `<` for the min update.

### T1 (P2). The two most load-bearing tests exercise code paths the game never uses
- `test_measure_judge_smoke.py` builds `IctusEvent`s by hand, leaving
  `max_derivative` at its `0.0` default. `game.py` passes
  `event.max_derivative or None`, so `0.0` → `None` → the **fallback**
  `peak/rise_duration` branch. The test therefore validates the legacy
  metric, not the shipped one. (Visible in its own output: a "Perfect"
  swing with peak 800 / rise 0.05 → 16,000 → "Low" → **Bunt**, a bucket
  it could never get on the real metric.)
- `test_ictus_intensity_invariance.py` reports a perfect 0.00ms spread
  across intensities, but its synthetic traces have a *zero baseline* and
  perfectly uniform dt. Both of the properties that would actually stress
  the detector on real data — a nonzero resting magnitude under the
  `drop_fraction` test, and jittery dt under `max_derivative` (S2) — are
  absent by construction. The clean pass is not evidence the real signal
  is invariant.

**Fix direction:** set `max_derivative` on the synthetic events (or drop
the `or None` idiom and pass it explicitly); add a baseline offset and
jittered dt to the invariance fixture and re-check the spread. These are
also the tests slated for porting in v3 Phase 2 — porting them as-is
would carry both blind spots into C#.

## P3 — Correctness, robustness, hygiene

### H1 (P3). `DEFAULT_OFFSET_S = 0.05` is *still* the v1 placeholder, and its comment now points at a phase that no longer exists
`calibration.py` line 12–16 still says "PLACEHOLDER … must be replaced
with a real measurement during Phase 3 hardware bring-up." Every fresh
install and every failed calibration runs on this guess, and it is
directly upstream of the felt-latency report. `Swinger_Build_Plan_v3.md`
Phase 0 already assigns this; flagging it here so it isn't lost.

### H2 (P3). The "~10,000-unit resting magnitude bias" recorded in the v1 Section 6 addendum looks wrong
That figure matches the **median** of the rate-contaminated `swings.csv`
(10,748) — i.e. it was measured on the pre-F3 file, and it's a median of
*active swinging*, not a resting measurement. Real resting magnitude in
the clean captures is far lower (`swings_counted.csv` p05 = 6, p10 = 53).
Since that number is recorded as the lead for the deferred per-axis
calibration work, it should be corrected before anyone acts on it.

### H3 (P3). `SessionLog` is never persisted
`export_csv()`/`export_json()` exist and are never called. The per-swing
record is the stated input to the mid-term feedback-loop goal *and* the
evidence Phase 0's latency investigation needs, but every round's data
dies with the process — including on the crash path in J2. Write it on
round end (and ideally on the QUIT path).

### H4 (P3). `PEAK_MIN_THRESHOLD = 200.0` is effectively dead
Real swing peaks are 20,000–33,000; resting is single digits to ~50. At
200 it filters nothing that `RISE_RATE_THRESHOLD` hasn't already caught.
Harmless today, but it's presented as a tunable safety net that isn't
doing the job described in the build plan — either raise it to something
meaningful (a few thousand) with evidence, or document it as vestigial.

### H5 (P3). Stale, contaminated `src/swings.csv` still sits beside the good captures
It's the pre-F3 unthrottled file (250,233 samples, 249,570 duplicates,
"25,023Hz"). Replaying it through the current detector yields
`max_derivative` values of 16.7M–71.9M — ~100× the real-capture range —
because dt collapses to microseconds. It has already caused one bad
derivation (H2) and it is the obvious wrong file for someone to reach for
when re-deriving S1. Move it to an `archive/` subfolder or delete it.

### H6 (P3). Detector loses one sample on every `REFRACTORY → ARMED` transition
The `REFRACTORY` branch returns early after flipping state to `ARMED`, so
the sample that ends the refractory period is never rise-tested. Costs up
to one sample (~15ms) of detection latency on rapid repeat swings.
Trivial, but free to fix while in there.

### H7 (P3). Post-round summary and scene are still below the v1 §4d spec
The summary shows only *means* (offset, sharpness) where §4d asks for the
per-swing scatter/list, and doesn't show the out count. `game.py` still
draws no batter or ball placeholder. Scope gaps rather than bugs, but
they're part of the "v0 prototype-ready" bar and Phase 5 will judge
against it.

### H8 (P3). `metronome._wait_until()` spins with a bare `pass`
The final ≤5ms before each beat is a tight no-yield spin on a daemon
thread — ~0.7% duty at 80 BPM, harmless on a normal desktop, but it can
starve the sampling loop on a single-core or heavily loaded machine and is
one of the few remaining candidates for *intermittent* timing weirdness.
`time.sleep(0)` inside the spin costs nothing and removes the risk.

## Suggested fix order for Sonnet

1. **G1** — judge immediately once an event is locked. Biggest felt-latency
   win, smallest diff, no behaviour change to the outcome.
2. **G3** — time-based render cadence (13fps → 60fps).
3. **G2** — derive the grace from the detector bound; clamp to
   `beat_interval/2` so BPM ≥ 120 can't silently break judging.
4. **J2 + J1** — make the loop time-driven rather than sample-driven,
   handle device loss, and stop opening a second HID handle for
   calibration. These gate any real playtest of the Settings flow.
5. **S2 then S1** — fix the sharpness metric's jitter sensitivity first,
   *then* re-derive the fallback range against it (deriving first would be
   wasted work). Needs a fresh controlled capture; hardware-gated.
6. **C1, D1, T1** — calibration seed/guard correctness, strict `<` in the
   min seek, and repairing the two tests so they stop passing for the
   wrong reason. **Do T1 before the v3 Phase 2 C# port**, or both blind
   spots get ported into the golden-trace baseline.
7. **H1–H8** — cleanup; H1 (`DEFAULT_OFFSET_S`) and H5 (stale CSV) should
   ride along with step 5's capture session, and H3 (persist the session
   log) is worth doing *before* the next playtest so the next latency
   report comes with data attached.

Note on sequencing: G1–G3 are all fixable and verifiable **without
hardware**, and together they change the felt latency by ~500ms. They
should land before the next playtest, so that whatever the player reports
afterwards is a fresh signal rather than a re-report of known,
already-diagnosed delay.
